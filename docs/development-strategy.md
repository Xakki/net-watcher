# Development strategy

## Chosen purpose and audience

Net Watcher will develop short, visual, synthetic lessons that teach researchers and teachers how to interpret network events without turning temporal association into causal explanation. The intended reader is an experienced engineer or researcher: basic networking is assumed, while subtle inference errors, source disagreement, measurement limits, and uncertainty are made explicit.

The first authorized deliverable is one static lesson on a registration-object change observed earlier than a route change. It demonstrates that ordering alone does not establish causation. It is learning material, not a research finding, product claim, or evidence that demand has been validated.

## Boundary and non-decisions

All current examples remain synthetic. They use relative times and no real IP, autonomous-system, registration-object, person, organization, or row-level identifiers. The current MIT license is unchanged. Monetization, future licensing, hosted features, analytics, and commercial reuse are not adopted. Live collection and broker work are deferred; this strategy authorizes neither source access nor deployment.

## Phases, outputs, and decision gates

**Phase 1 — synthetic lesson.** Produce one accessible static case with three clocks, competing explanations, an evidence audit, a defensible conclusion, and a worked self-check. Acceptance requires clear separation of claimed times from observable times, no causal or intent attribution, and passing repository and site safety checks. The owner decides whether the lesson is sufficiently useful and safe to continue.

**Phase 2 — bounded educational review.** Invite a small, explicitly approved review of the lesson using only the public artifact and synthetic fixture. Record no visitor analytics or personal research data. Acceptance requires reviewers to identify the tempting inference, name the missing evidence, and explain why the conclusion remains observational. The owner decides whether to revise, pause, or prepare another synthetic case.

**Phase 3 — lesson set.** Add further synthetic cases only after the Phase 2 gate. Each case must have a distinct inference trap, declared limits, accessible presentation, and a maintenance owner. The owner decides whether the set earns a separate research or education proposal; no live source work follows automatically.

## Demand validation without analytics or unsolicited outreach

Educational demand may be assessed through explicitly approved, consent-based review sessions, classroom or workshop pilots, and requests that people choose to submit through the public correction channel. Evaluation should use task completion, explanation quality, confusion points, and requests for another lesson rather than page views, tracking, funnels, or inferred identities. No unsolicited outreach, covert measurement, account requirement, or collection of personal data is part of this strategy. These signals remain hypotheses, not proof of market demand.

## Stop rules

Stop or revise if readers cannot distinguish a claimed timestamp from an observation, infer causation from ordering after the worked explanation, or identify at least one competing explanation. Stop if accessibility, synthetic-only guarantees, or the no-analytics boundary cannot be maintained. Stop before any real source access, broker implementation, hosted operation, monetization, or licensing change; each requires a separate written decision, scope, review, and authorization.

The next decision is whether the first lesson meets the educational acceptance criteria. Until that decision is made, Net Watcher remains a static, public, synthetic-only draft with no active study and no published findings.
